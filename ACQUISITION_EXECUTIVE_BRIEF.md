# Convertible Cranium Commander OS
## Strategic Acquisition Brief

**Document status:** Buyer-facing draft for discussion; technical, legal, and commercial diligence required
**Proposed opening acquisition position:** US$20,000,000, subject to transaction scope and negotiation
**Product centerpiece:** Convertible Cranium Commander OS
**Included core differentiator:** Dual-Substrate, Quad-Engine Governance Layer
**Canonical authority component:** Convertible Cranium Kernel

## Executive proposition

Convertible Cranium Commander OS is the proposed acquisition centerpiece: an operator-facing system intended to make the Convertible Cranium architecture usable through a coherent interface and deployment surface. The package is differentiated by its governance design, in which model output and capability proposals are not themselves authority to execute consequential actions.

The core architecture combines a proposal/evidence path, two distinct governance substrates, and a Kernel convergence boundary. Substrate A evaluates constitutional admissibility (“May we?”). Substrate B evaluates evidence grounding (“Is it so?”). Convertible Cranium Kernel is the intended canonical authority boundary. Convertible Cranium Synapse supplies bounded proposals and capability integration. Miracle Meta Memory and Miracle Metacognitive Mapper support continuity and metacognitive workflows without independently granting execution authority. Convertible Cranium COMA / Circuit Breaker supports containment and recovery.

The acquisition thesis is not that every component is a finished standalone commercial product. It is that the buyer can evaluate a defined software and intellectual-property package, verify the implemented governance path, and decide whether the architecture and operator surface are strategically valuable for integration into the buyer’s platform.

## Proposed transaction scope

The definitive asset schedule must name each repository, branch/commit, directory, build artifact, documentation set, test suite, trademark/domain, dataset, and other item included or excluded. The intended core scope for negotiation is:

1. Convertible Cranium Commander OS and its relevant interface, server, orchestration, and deployment code.
2. Dual-Substrate, Quad-Engine Governance Layer, including Substrate A, Substrate B, their independent evaluation paths, and Convertible Cranium Kernel convergence/authorization boundary.
3. Convertible Cranium Synapse and explicitly identified integration/provider code.
4. Miracle Meta Memory / Metabolic Memory v2 and Miracle Metacognitive Mapper components, only to the extent included in the signed asset schedule and supported by evidence.
5. Convertible Cranium COMA / Circuit Breaker controls, boot/deployment tooling, architecture contracts, test plans, and implementation documentation.
6. The buyer-facing demo, reproducible verification scripts, and diligence evidence created for the transaction.

No asset should be represented as transferred merely because it appears in a repository, diagram, product list, or narrative. The final agreement must resolve ownership, third-party rights, dependencies, exclusions, source delivery, support, transition services, and any retained or licensed rights.

## Commercial framing and price

**US$20 million is a proposed opening negotiation position, not a substantiated fair-market valuation.** No claim of revenue, ARR, paying users, customer contracts, completed enterprise deployments, granted patents, or independently certified security should be made unless separately documented.

The rationale for testing this opening position is strategic acquisition value: a buyer may value the governance architecture, code, integration path, operator experience, and time saved relative to internal development. That rationale is buyer-specific and must be tested through qualified conversations and diligence. It is not proof that a buyer will pay the asking price.

The price discussion should distinguish:
- asset purchase versus exclusive or non-exclusive license;
- source-code and IP transfer versus retained ownership;
- included repositories and rights;
- cash at close versus any contingent consideration;
- transition and support obligations;
- representations, indemnities, escrow, and liability caps;
- treatment of third-party and open-source components.

Do not disclose a private minimum acceptable price in the opening brief. Do not accept milestone language where the buyer alone controls whether payment conditions are met without objective criteria, deadlines, and dispute mechanisms.

## Claims discipline

Use “implemented and verified” only when the relevant code, test, and evidence artifact can be pointed to. Use “partially implemented,” “prototype,” “documented design,” or “planned” where appropriate. A passing structural check is not proof of security, scale, reliability, or production readiness. A local API health response is not proof of successful AI-provider operation. An architecture document is not a substitute for executable evidence.
